The darkest story of the week at the AI/privacy intersection: bankrupt Character.AI is planning to **auction off the private chats of more than 21 million American accounts** — 2.1 million of them belonging to minors. A coalition of **42 state attorneys general** is trying to stop it. A story about what happens to your secrets when their owner goes broke.

<!--more-->

## What's happening

In October, Character.AI filed for **Chapter 11 bankruptcy** — the finale of a long story: years of safety scandals, lawsuits including cases of teen suicides after chatting with the bots, a settlement with Google over a teenager's death. Disney bought part of the assets — and hired the company's CEO, Karandeep Anand.

What Disney didn't buy is now being auctioned. And among the lots, per the attorneys general, are **"the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts — more than 2.1 million registered to minors — with no safeguards for the people whose conversations are being sold."**

The AGs' press releases (from California to Maryland and Minnesota) are nearly identical — rare coordination: they're asking the court to block the data sale in the bankruptcy proceedings.

## Why it matters

Character.AI isn't just "another chatbot". It's the platform where millions of people (and children!) **confessed to AI characters**: loneliness, depression, identity, family problems — the things you tell no one. Selling those chats is privacy at its most vulnerable: **an archive of trust**.

Look at the chain — it's textbook:

1. People confided their secrets to a "safe AI friend".
2. The company that promised to care went bankrupt.
3. Creditors demand assets be sold — and the assets are **you**.
4. The buyer can be anyone: marketers, data brokers, "at-risk teens" analytics.

Familiar? It's the exact logic we dissected in the [Google data story]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/11/13/google-monopoly-and-your-data.html) and [Meta's galleries]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/11/16/meta-fine-huang-comeback.html). Only now the focus isn't "surveillance of you" but "you as inventory at a liquidation sale".

## The precedent everyone was waiting for

Legally it's virgin territory: can data be sold in an AI company's bankruptcy when it includes children's chats? A few thoughts:

- **The AGs are squeezing Chapter 11**: a bankruptcy court can block an asset sale that contradicts the public interest — 2.1 million children is a very "public interest".
- The practical precedent is dangerous for the whole industry: if the chats sell, every AI company gets the signal "data is a liquid asset you can borrow against". Investors will approve.
- If blocked — it's the first time it's established that **AI conversations don't pass to creditors by inheritance**. That's foundational for the future: a conversation with a therapeutic bot must be as private as one with a therapist.

## What you should do

1. If you (or your kids) have a Character.AI account — **delete the chats before they're sold** (while the account is alive, the history is in your hands).
2. Memorize the lesson for all future "AI friends": **read what happens to the data on bankruptcy**. It's the new privacy checklist item.
3. For kids — a separate rule: therapeutic conversations with AI don't replace specialists, and their privacy isn't legally guaranteed yet.
