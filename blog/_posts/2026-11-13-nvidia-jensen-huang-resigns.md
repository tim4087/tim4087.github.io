On November 12, 2026, something that seemed impossible just two weeks earlier happened: **Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and board member of NVIDIA** — the company he co-founded 33 years ago and turned into the "central bank of AI", worth around $4 trillion. A career built over decades collapsed in less than two weeks.

## Timeline of the scandal

**Sunday, November 8.** NVIDIA's board announced an investigation into Huang over reports linking him to a Chinese intelligence-gathering effort. Huang himself published a message to employees that day: *"I owe my family and all of you an apology. I was unfaithful to my wife."* The stock dropped roughly 10% over two days.

**November 10.** The independent board members retained former acting U.S. Attorney Joon H. Kim and the firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct "a thorough and independent investigation".

**November 12.** Huang resigned — effective immediately, from all posts. CFO **Colette Kress** was named interim CEO; Harvey C. Jones became the company's first-ever Chair of the Board. Shares fell another ~5% on the news — down 11% for the week.

## What is actually alleged

According to AFP and ABC News, the story involves Huang's affair with **Alice Huang** — a former executive at data center company Megaspeed, which is under investigation in Singapore and which, per the reports, was outed as an alleged spy for Chinese intelligence.

Important: these are **allegations, not a verdict** — the investigation continues, and no official charges have been filed against anyone. But with a CEO's access to strategic information at stake, the board acted decisively.

Details that add color:

- Financial Times describes NVIDIA under Huang as a "feudal system" — with the founder's daughter and son-in-law in powerful positions;
- WSJ recalled that in August, Huang publicly called Kress "a deer in the headlights" and "a reporting CFO, not a strategy partner" — she now leads the company;
- Reuters reported a third affair — the investigation is widening;
- Congressional Democrats have launched their own probe into Huang's "scheme" with Trump.

## What it means for NVIDIA and the industry

**Short term — nerves.** The Q3 FY2027 earnings report lands on November 17, as scheduled, and will be the most intriguing in years: for the first time in 33 years, it won't be Huang presenting. NVIDIA is the world's second-largest company by market cap after Apple; its shares sit in index funds (and, likely, in someone's 401(k)), so an 11% drop is felt far beyond Wall Street.

**Medium term — continuity.** The board stresses that operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap are unchanged. Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are scaling with Oracle to multi-gigawatt scale; the sovereign AI partnership with Rebellions and SK Telecom in Korea was announced November 9 — right in the middle of the scandal. The wheel keeps turning.

**Long term — an open question.** Huang wasn't just a CEO, he was the living symbol of the AI boom: the leather jacket, the GTC keynotes, "the more you buy, the more you save". TechCrunch is already asking what happens to NVIDIA's open-source commitment — Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue publicly vowed to "keep fighting for open source AI". An interim CEO is not a 5–10 year answer. The succession search will be the industry's biggest storyline.

## A breakout moment: AMD, Broadcom and the rest

The market reacted instantly — on resignation day, traders literally **rotated money out of NVIDIA and into its competitors**:

| Company | Nov 12 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| NVIDIA | **−5%** (intraday low $148.80) | Leadership uncertainty |
| AMD | **+5–9.5%** | Most direct AI-accelerator rival |
| Broadcom | **+5–5.9%** | Custom AI silicon + data-center networking |
| Alphabet | **+5%** | In-house TPUs |
| Intel | **−1.5%** | Sank with the whole sector |
| CoreWeave (NVIDIA partner) | **−7.5%** | Hostage of the NVIDIA ecosystem |

The main contenders for the throne:

- **AMD** — the closest pursuer. Quarterly data-center revenue hit a record **$8.5B (+97% YoY)** on the Instinct MI450 wave. But NVIDIA is still ~10x its size: **$906B market cap versus $4.2T**. AMD has a shot, but it needs years of flawless execution — and their moment is exactly now.
- **Broadcom** — Wall Street's quiet favorite: **$16.7B quarterly AI-chip revenue (+221%)**. It builds custom accelerators for the giants (including Google's TPUs) — meaning it wins in any scenario where customers want **non-NVIDIA**.
- **Google (TPU)** — unveiled two new chip systems on Monday, targeting privacy-sensitive and memory-intensive workloads. If NVIDIA stumbles, hyperscalers may seriously consider a full migration to their own silicon for the first time.
- **Intel** — not in the game yet: Gaudi 3 flopped; hopes rest on the Crescent Island inference chip.
- **China (Huawei, Cambricon)** — a separate story: NVIDIA chips are already **piling up in Hong Kong** because China withholds import approval. Every NVIDIA scandal accelerates China's self-sufficiency.

The key number for context: per Morgan Stanley (March 2026), NVIDIA held **~85% of AI-processor revenue**, custom ASICs just over 10%, AMD under 5%. That gap isn't closed in a quarter. But for the first time in years, customers have a **reason** (not a desire — a reason) to diversify, and that changes the game more than any benchmark.

## What happens next

1. **November 17 — NVIDIA's earnings.** The first in 33 years without Huang. Kress has already promised: "We will continue advancing the work that has made NVIDIA a leader in accelerated computing and AI." Watch two numbers: Vera Rubin execution and next-quarter guidance — any soft "transition period" language and the market will punish it instantly.
2. **The succession search.** Bloomberg expects a "quite conservative" leader after this experience — an operational manager, not a visionary showman. Lower risk, but part of the brand's magic disappears.
3. **Customers re-examine contracts.** Not because the chips got worse — they didn't. But buyers who for years heard "nobody but NVIDIA" are, for the first time, sitting down with AMD and Broadcom with real budgets. The rotation that started on the stock market will reach contracts within a quarter or two.
4. **China turns away for good.** While NVIDIA searches for a new CEO, Huawei and Cambricon get precious months without price and technology pressure.
5. **Open source in question.** Nemotron, the CUDA ecosystem, the OpenShell donation — that was all Huang's personal policy. A new conservative CEO cuts "off-profile" spending first, and NVIDIA's open-source bet is left hanging.

## What it means for AI as a whole

Bloomberg put it bluntly: **"The Nvidia scandal creates risk for the entire AI industry, not just its CEO."** NVIDIA is the "central bank of AI": nearly every lab's compute runs on its silicon. When the central bank has a fever, everyone does.

But the doomsday scenario — "AI stops" — won't happen, and here's why: the giants' capex doesn't depend on news about anyone's private life — Nebius plans **$40B** in investment, SpaceX is asking for **$40B** for NVIDIA hardware, Oracle is scaling clusters to multi-gigawatt levels. Demand for compute outweighs any scandal.

The real consequences for AI will be subtler and more interesting:

- **Diversification gets pricier, not chips.** Labs will spend on porting to ROCm (AMD) and TPUs — slower optimizations in the short term, but a more resilient industry. The irony: the scandal will strengthen the very AI infrastructure it seemed to threaten.
- **Sovereign AI accelerates.** Korea (Rebellions + SK Telecom already on NVIDIA NVLink Fusion), China, Europe — everyone now wants "their own" while the American leader gets back on its feet.
- **A new leadership model.** The era of "icons" — Huang in his leather jacket, Altman in his sweater — seems to be ending. The era of operational caution begins. For an industry growing 200% a quarter, that may be useful maturity. May be.

## Takeaways

1. **The "lone genius" bet has failed.** Trillion-dollar companies shouldn't depend on one person's reputation — but they did. Investors have already repriced it: $1.27 trillion in market cap lost in the month before the resignation.
2. **The board's speed is remarkable.** From first reports to resignation — 11 days. NVIDIA's board showed that governance sometimes actually works.
3. **For the first time, the AI industry is stress-testing people, not models.** And judging by the rotation into AMD and Broadcom, the market believes the throne has opened up more than it seems.

## P.S. My two cents

The board, to its credit, played it textbook: check — an 11-day investigation, checkmate — an immediate resignation. But here's my prediction: NVIDIA will survive this. The company rests not on a leather jacket but on CUDA and the simple absence of a real alternative. What won't survive is the myth of the infallible founder. Moral of the story: infrastructure outlives your private life; reputation doesn't.
