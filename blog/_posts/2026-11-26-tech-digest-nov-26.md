The week in brief: X finally got parental controls, Amazon lost its "we're just logistics" battle, Google brought face recognition to $35 cameras, and NYT photographers were warned off a Photoshop update. All of it — with takeaways.

<!--more-->

## 👨‍👩‍👧 X finally gets parental controls

The platform's first such tools ever (current name X, formerly Twitter):

- parents can see **how much time the teen spent in the app** and set daily limits;
- they can **require approval for new followers**;
- "For You" recommendations can be limited to accounts the teen already follows.

What matters and what's nice: parents **cannot read direct messages, search or viewing history** — and every change is visible to the teen. In spirit it's a time-budget control, not surveillance. Note: TikTok and Instagram have had this for ages — X was just closing an old debt.

## 📦 Amazon loses to CPSC: "you're a distributor"

A Maryland court sided with the Consumer Product Safety Commission: Amazon must **carry out recalls of more than 400,000 hazardous products** sold via Fulfilled by Amazon — from carbon monoxide detectors that don't detect to hair dryers without electrocution protection.

Amazon's "we're just logistics" argument was rejected: **whoever sells, answers**. The precedent widens marketplace liability for third-party goods — welcome to the new reality, Ozon and Wildberries.

## 📹 Face recognition — now for $35

Google Home brought its **Familiar Faces** feature (recognizing "familiar faces") to Walmart's budget cameras: the $35 Outdoor Camera Plug-In and the $77 Floodlight. A Google Home Premium subscription is required. The app also gained a button to remotely trigger a **30-second security siren**.

The tech that yesterday was expensive Nest exotica is today a Walmart shelf. Convenient? Very. Privacy? Recall our [Google data post]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/11/13/google-monopoly-and-your-data.html) — the same company that knows your email is now teaching cameras to recognize your family. Your call.

## 📸 NYT: don't update Photoshop (yet)

The New York Times formally warned its photographers: **don't move to the new Photoshop version** because of its AI features — until the newsroom figures out how they affect the evidentiary value of news photos. Meanwhile, the Binding of Isaac developer **cut the AI-generated music** from Repentance Plus. Two small signs of a big drama: the content industry is learning to roll back AI where trust costs more than speed.
