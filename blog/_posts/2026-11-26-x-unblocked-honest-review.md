## Backstory: banned for nothing

- **Early 2026.** I register on X. Two days of browsing — and I'm banned. No warning, no explanation: I couldn't have done anything — no spam, no rudeness, the account was empty.
- **Three appeals.** All rejected with automated replies. A classic: X has long fought bots, and its registration-time anti-spam filters churn out false positives. The system runs on "guilty until proven innocent" — and there's no human support for ordinary accounts to prove it to.
- **I dropped it.** For many months.
- **Late 2026.** I sent two more appeals — and one finally reached a human being. The account came back.

Part one's moral: if X banned you "for nothing" — **don't give up after the first rejections**. My path took five appeals and a year, but the account is alive.

## What it's really like: a cold shower

So I came back — and here's what I see, honestly, no sugar:

- **Reach is zero.** Posts are seen by a few people. Not "few" — literally zero: the algorithm just doesn't show new accounts.
- **Nothing takes off.** Reposts, likes, links — silence.
- **Less interesting content.** The For You feed is big accounts, hype cycles and AI slop. Living discussions moved into comments on big posts and communities.

## Why it's not just me

I dug into the 2026 algorithm — and my zero stats suddenly made sense:

1. **Cold start is a dead zone.** The X feed is built on *predicted engagement*: the system looks at your interaction history and decides who to show you to. A new (or post-ban) account has no history → nothing to predict → no impressions → no engagement → an even worse forecast. A vicious circle, and it's official policy, not a conspiracy.
2. **Links cut reach.** Hardly a secret now: posts with external links are deprioritized — X wants to keep people on-platform. My first instinct, "post a link to my blog", is exactly what buries a post.
3. **The first hour decides everything.** If a post doesn't gather engagement within ~60 minutes, it never escapes a narrow circle. A newbie with 20 followers has no one to gather it from.
4. **For You is a closed club.** Since the rebrand, ranking favors big accounts with a proven engagement history. The entry threshold has multiplied compared to "Twitter as we knew it".

## What actually works: 10 rules from zero in 2026

Collected from fresh guides and my own experience — this is my plan, sharing it:

1. **Replies are the beginner's main weapon.** Thoughtful (not "agreed!") comments on big accounts in your niche — in the **first hour** after they post. Your reply is seen by thousands of the post's readers: the only legal "borrowed reach".
2. **A niche beats "about everything".** The algorithm predicts by topic. An account "about AI, privacy and startups" gets predictable signals; "football and memes too" is noise that doesn't rank.
3. **No links in the post body.** The link goes into the first reply or your bio. The post text must hook on its own.
4. **The first line is the hook.** The feed shows only the first 30-40 characters. Hook first, substance after, deep-dive in a thread.
5. **Threads over walls of text.** Numbered threads keep attention (dwell time is one of the top ranking signals) and give the algorithm reasons to show the next post.
6. **Video and GIFs.** A gaze lingering on media weighs more than likes. A 10-second clip beats a paragraph.
7. **Rhythm beats bursts.** 1-2 posts a day + 15-20 replies daily. The algorithm loves a constant signal; weeks of silence reset progress.
8. **Communities are the underrated entrance.** Community feeds are alive and chronological: you can be noticed there without a thousand followers.
9. **Premium — later.** The paid subscription genuinely widens reach, but boosting content that doesn't work organically is amplifying emptiness. Rules 1-8 first.
10. **Blog synergy.** Every post on this blog is a ready-made thread. The formula: post's thesis on X → 5-8 tweets of breakdown → link to the full post in the final reply. That's how nearly every techblogger I admire grew.
